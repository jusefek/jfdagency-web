Why Transport & 3PL Providers Face Strict NIS2 Enforcement

Directive (EU) 2022/2555 classifies road, rail, maritime, and air transport operators—along with ports and intelligent transport systems—under Annex I (Sectors of High Criticality). Even medium-sized freight forwarders, 3PL warehouses, and last-mile carriers that fall below direct size thresholds are pulled into scope via Article 21(2)(d) when working with multinational shippers and port authorities.

Because logistics relies heavily on EDI integrations, TMS/WMS portals, and automated customs exchanges, a ransomware outage or spoofed dispatch email at a subcontractor can halt an entire manufacturing supply chain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do small hauliers and customs brokers have to comply with NIS2?

If you are below medium-enterprise thresholds you may not be directly regulated, but Annex I carriers and shippers must audit their direct logistics subcontractors under Art. 21(2)(d).

What happens if a 3PL suffers a ransomware attack?

Under Art. 23 and commercial SLAs, affected partners expect an early warning within 24 hours and an impact assessment within 72 hours.
