NIS2 Incident Reporting (Art. 23): Mastering the 24h Early Warning & 72h Assessment

NIS2 imposes one of the tightest breach-reporting clocks in EU law: 24 hours for an early warning. Enterprise buyers pass that same urgency into supplier SLAs.

Why Suppliers Must Have a 24h / 72h Escalation Plan Ready

Article 23 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555 requires essential and important entities to notify their national CSIRT (such as BSI in Germany, ANSSI in France, or INCIBE-CERT in Spain) within 24 hours of becoming aware of a significant incident, followed by a formal assessment within 72 hours and a final report within 1 month.

Because regulated buyers cannot meet a 24-hour statutory deadline if their IT, SaaS, or logistics vendors take days to disclose an outage or breach, procurement contracts now mandate 12h–24h vendor notification SLAs and ask for a documented Incident Notification Policy during onboarding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we need full root-cause analysis within 24 hours?

No. The 24-hour early warning is a brief initial alert; technical assessment and IoCs follow within 72 hours.

Does a supplier notify the national CSIRT or the enterprise client?

If the supplier is not directly regulated, it notifies the affected enterprise client immediately under contract so the client can meet its Art. 23 CSIRT deadline.
