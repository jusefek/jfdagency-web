NIS2 Fines, Penalties & Commercial Consequences Across the European Union

For regulated entities, NIS2 brings GDPR-scale fines and personal management liability. For B2B suppliers, the immediate risk is losing enterprise contracts.

Statutory Fines vs. Commercial Supply-Chain Exclusion

Article 34 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555 sets maximum administrative fines of at least €10,000,000 or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover for essential entities, and €7,000,000 or 1.4% of turnover for important entities. Furthermore, Article 20 introduces personal accountability for management bodies to approve and oversee cybersecurity risk measures.

While national regulators focus direct audits on essential and important entities, those entities protect themselves from fines by enforcing strict cybersecurity clauses across their supply chain. For an SME vendor, failing a NIS2 assessment means failed tenders, frozen vendor onboarding, and non-renewal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can company directors be held personally liable under NIS2?

Yes. Article 20 requires management bodies to approve risk-management measures and oversee implementation, with potential liability for non-compliance.

Why would a small supplier worry about NIS2 fines?

Even if not directly fined by a regulator, suppliers face commercial exclusion and contractual indemnity clauses from regulated buyers.
