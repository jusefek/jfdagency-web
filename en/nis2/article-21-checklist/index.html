The 10 Mandatory Risk Measures Under Article 21(2) NIS2

Article 21(2) of Directive (EU) 2022/2555 defines ten core cybersecurity risk-management measures that regulated entities and their critical suppliers must address. While several controls are organizational (such as staff training and business continuity), three technical pillars are immediately visible from the outside via your corporate domain.

Automated vendor-risk scoring tools inspect your public DNS, mail authentication, TLS handshake, and HTTP response headers within seconds. If your domain scores poorly on these external vectors, your supplier onboarding request is flagged for manual security review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which HTTP security headers matter most for NIS2 vendor scoring?

Strict-Transport-Security (HSTS), Content-Security-Policy (CSP), X-Content-Type-Options, and X-Frame-Options are the primary headers checked by B2B risk scanners.

Is DMARC with p=none enough for enterprise buyers?

p=none is useful for initial monitoring, but procurement and CISO teams increasingly expect p=quarantine or p=reject to actively block domain spoofing.

How often should we re-verify our Article 21 domain posture?

At least annually or whenever you change DNS providers, email relays, or hosting infrastructure.
